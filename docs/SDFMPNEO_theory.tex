\begin{abstract}
This document fixes the theoretical baseline of SDF-MPNEO for underwater wireless power transfer after removal of the fluid-dynamics branch. The retained physics are electromagnetic and thermal, while conductor, package, seawater, and other spatial media remain explicitly represented. The objective is to map geometry, constant operating conditions, an initial thermal state, and an arbitrary query time directly to the thermal state and electromagnetic quantities without transient time stepping and without labelled Maxwell, finite-element, or experimental solution data in training. The central construction is an analytic neural network whose neurons are themselves closed-form responses of first-order dissipative operators. Spatial reduction is deterministic and physics-derived; network topology grows from the unresolved physical residual; and thermal-reduction, neural, and electromagnetic numerical errors are combined in a single a posteriori certification framework. All approximation orders are selected by certified error requirements rather than empirical rules.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, notation, and non-empirical design rules}

\subsection{Current scope}
The present theory considers the coupled system
\[
\boxed{\text{electromagnetics}\;\longleftrightarrow\;\text{heat transfer}}
\]
for an underwater wireless power transfer device. Fluid velocity is not a state variable. Seawater is nevertheless retained as a spatial electromagnetic and thermal medium.

The physical domain is partitioned as
\[
\Omega(\mupar)=\Omega_{\mathrm c}(\mupar)\cup
\Omega_{\mathrm p}(\mupar)\cup
\Omega_{\mathrm s}(\mupar),
\]
where the subscripts denote conductor, package, and seawater, respectively. Additional passive dielectric or insulating subdomains can be included without changing the theory.

The parameter vector is
\[
\mupar=(\mupar_g,\mupar_u)\in\mathcal P,
\]
where \(\mupar_g\) contains geometry and material parameters and \(\mupar_u\) contains fixed operating conditions such as excitation frequency, source voltage or current, load, and ambient temperature. The external conditions are constant for \(0\le t\le t_q\). The query time \(t_q\) is arbitrary.

\subsection{Required online map}
For initial thermal state \(T_0\), the online operator must evaluate
\[
(\mupar,T_0,t_q)
\mapsto
\left\{
T(\bm x,t_q),
\mathbf Z(t_q),
R(t_q),L(t_q),M(t_q),
P_{\mathrm{Cu}}(t_q),P_{\mathrm{sea}}(t_q)
\right\}
\]
without integrating the thermal equations from \(0\) to \(t_q\).

\subsection{Non-empirical rules}
The following are method constraints, not preferences.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item No labelled electromagnetic, thermal, finite-element, computational-fluid, or experimental solution is used to train the neural evolution model.
\item No empirical near/far spatial split is permitted.
\item No empirical fixed reduced dimension is permitted. Reduced dimensions are selected from certified output-error requirements.
\item No empirical neural depth or width is permitted. Analytic neurons are added by a residual-driven enrichment criterion and stopped by certification.
\item No empirical thermal time constant is introduced. Decay rates originate from the deterministic thermal spectrum.
\item No manually weighted sum of unrelated training losses is used as the primary correctness criterion. The primary neural residual is measured in a physical dual/energy norm.
\item Numerical electromagnetic compression or quadrature is accepted only together with an error estimator that enters the total certificate.
\item A model is not extrapolated beyond its certified physical/parameter domain merely because the neural network returns a finite number.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Full electromagnetic--thermal problem}

\subsection{Thermal problem}
Let \(T(\bm x,t)\) denote temperature. In each material subdomain,
\begin{equation}
\rho c_p\,\partial_t T-\nabla\cdot(k\nabla T)=Q_{\mathrm{em}}(T;\mupar)
\qquad \text{in }\Omega(\mupar).
\label{eq:heat_full}
\end{equation}
At an internal interface \(\Gamma_{ij}\), perfect thermal contact gives
\begin{align}
T_i&=T_j,\\
k_i\nabla T_i\cdot\bm n_i&=-k_j\nabla T_j\cdot\bm n_j.
\end{align}
Non-perfect contact can be introduced through a physically specified interfacial conductance and is incorporated in the weak form.

Boundary conditions are physical model inputs. A Dirichlet condition \(T=T_\infty\), a Robin condition
\[
-k\nabla T\cdot\bm n=h(T-T_\infty),
\]
or an infinite-domain treatment may be used. The outer-domain location or boundary coefficient is not an empirical tuning variable: it must be prescribed from the physical environment or selected by an independently verified domain-truncation error bound.

\subsection{Electromagnetic problem}
At each thermal state, the electromagnetic dynamics are assumed fast relative to the thermal dynamics and are represented by a deterministic harmonic operator
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{em}}(T;\mupar)\,\bm u_{\mathrm{em}}
=\bm b_{\mathrm{em}}(\mupar),
\label{eq:em_operator}
\end{equation}
where \(\bm u_{\mathrm{em}}\) denotes the electromagnetic state required by the selected BFZI/DSE-backed formulation. Temperature modifies, at minimum,
\begin{align}
\rho_{\mathrm{Cu}}&=\rho_{\mathrm{Cu}}(T),\\
\sigma_{\mathrm{sea}}&=\sigma_{\mathrm{sea}}(T).
\end{align}
The deterministic electromagnetic backend returns
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E(T;\mupar)
=\left(
\mathbf Z,
P_{\mathrm{Cu}},P_{\mathrm{sea}},
Q_{\mathrm{em}}
\right).
\label{eq:em_outputs}
\end{equation}
The local source \(Q_{\mathrm{em}}\) contains conductor and seawater losses. In a harmonic convention using peak complex fields, for example,
\[
Q_{\mathrm{sea}}(\bm x)=\frac12\sigma_{\mathrm{sea}}(T)|\bm E(\bm x)|^2,
\]
with the precise prefactor determined by the phasor convention used consistently in the backend.

\subsection{Fast/slow coupling}
The multirate assumption is
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{em}}\ll\tau_{\mathrm{th}}.
\]
This does not decouple the physics. At each slow thermal state,
\[
T\longrightarrow
\{\rho_{\mathrm{Cu}}(T),\sigma_{\mathrm{sea}}(T)\}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal E
\longrightarrow
Q_{\mathrm{em}}
\longrightarrow
T.
\]
The electromagnetic state is harmonic/quasi-steady at the instantaneous thermal state, while the thermal state evolves continuously.

\section{Unified geometry and a parameter-consistent thermal coordinate system}

\subsection{Reference-domain mapping}
Let \(\widehat\Omega\) be a fixed reference domain containing reference conductor, package, and seawater subdomains. For each admissible geometry, define
\begin{equation}
F_{\mupar}:\widehat\Omega\rightarrow\Omega(\mupar).
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Uniform geometric regularity]
For every \(\mupar\in\mathcal P\), \(F_{\mupar}\) is bi-Lipschitz and
\[
0<J_-\le |\det DF_{\mupar}(\widehat{\bm x})|\le J_+<\infty,
\]
with uniformly bounded \(DF_{\mupar}\) and \(DF_{\mupar}^{-1}\).
\end{assumption}

After pullback to \(\widehat\Omega\), all parameter instances act on one function space \(V\). Define the thermal mass and stiffness forms
\begin{align}
m_{\mupar}(u,v)&=\int_{\widehat\Omega}
\widehat\rho\widehat c_p\,uv\,J_{\mupar}\,d\widehat\Omega,\\
k_{\mupar}(u,v)&=\int_{\widehat\Omega}
\left(DF_{\mupar}^{-T}\nabla u\right)^T
\widehat k
\left(DF_{\mupar}^{-T}\nabla v\right)
J_{\mupar}\,d\widehat\Omega,
\end{align}
with interface and boundary terms included where required.

\begin{assumption}[Uniform thermal coercivity]
There exist constants \(m_->0\) and \(k_->0\) such that, on the homogeneous trial space,
\[
m_{\mupar}(v,v)\ge m_-\norm{v}_{L^2}^2,
\qquad
k_{\mupar}(v,v)\ge k_-\norm{v}_{V}^2
\]
for all \(\mupar\in\mathcal P\).
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Deterministic thermal spectrum}
After spatial discretization on the reference domain, define
\begin{equation}
\Kmat(\mupar)\bm\phi_i(\mupar)
=\lambda_i(\mupar)\Mmat(\mupar)\bm\phi_i(\mupar),
\label{eq:eig}
\end{equation}
with
\[
\bm\phi_i^T\Mmat\bm\phi_j=\delta_{ij},
\qquad
0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots.
\]
These eigenvalues are physical thermal decay rates. No artificial time scale is introduced.

\subsection{Spectral clusters instead of individual eigenvectors}
Individual eigenvectors are not reliable coordinates across geometry changes because eigenvalue crossings or multiplicities can rotate the eigenvectors. Therefore the primary object is an isolated spectral subspace.

Let \(\mathcal C_r(\mupar)\) be the spectral cluster retained by the reduced model and let \(\Gamma\) be a contour enclosing exactly that cluster. Its spectral projector is
\begin{equation}
P_r(\mupar)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}
\oint_\Gamma (z\I-\mathcal A_{\mathrm th}(\mupar))^{-1}\,dz,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{\mathrm th}=\Mmat^{-1}\Kmat.
\label{eq:spectral_projector}
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Spectral separation]
The selected cluster is separated from the discarded spectrum:
\[
\delta_r(\mupar)=
\dist\left(\mathcal C_r(\mupar),
\sigma(\mathcal A_{\mathrm th})\setminus\mathcal C_r(\mupar)\right)>0
\]
on the certified parameter domain. If eigenvalues cross inside the selected cluster, the entire crossing cluster is retained.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Path-independent canonical basis}
To remove sign, permutation, and rotation ambiguity, choose a fixed reference anchor matrix \(B\) with \(r\) columns and define
\begin{equation}
G_B(\mupar)=B^T\Mmat(\mupar)P_r(\mupar)B.
\end{equation}
When \(G_B\) is positive definite, define the canonical basis
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\PhiT_r(\mupar)=P_r(\mupar)B\,G_B(\mupar)^{-1/2}.
}
\label{eq:canonical_basis}
\end{equation}
Then
\[
\PhiT_r^T\Mmat\PhiT_r=\I.
\]
This construction is deterministic and path-independent.

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate continuity]
If \(F_{\mupar}\), \(\Mmat(\mupar)\), and \(\Kmat(\mupar)\) depend continuously on \(\mupar\), the retained cluster is isolated, and \(G_B(\mupar)\) remains positive definite, then \(P_r(\mupar)\) and the canonical basis in \eqref{eq:canonical_basis} vary continuously with \(\mupar\). If these dependencies are analytic, the projector and basis are analytic away from loss of cluster isolation or anchor rank.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Continuity of the resolvent on the isolating contour implies continuity of the Riesz projector \eqref{eq:spectral_projector}. Positive definiteness of \(G_B\) makes the principal inverse square root continuous. Their composition gives continuity of \(\PhiT_r\). The analytic statement follows from analytic perturbation of isolated spectral projectors and analyticity of the principal matrix square root on the positive-definite cone.
\end{proof}

If the anchor loses rank, the parameter domain is covered by an atlas of anchor charts. Chart validity is determined by a certified lower bound on the smallest singular value of the overlap matrix, not by a manually chosen geometric region. Transition matrices between valid charts are computed explicitly.

\section{Deterministic thermal reduction}

\subsection{Boundary lifting and reduced state}
Let \(T_b(\mupar)\) satisfy the nonhomogeneous thermal boundary conditions. Write
\begin{equation}
T(\bm x,t;\mupar)=T_b(\bm x;\mupar)
+\PhiT_r(\bm x;\mupar)\aT(t;\mupar).
\label{eq:T_rom}
\end{equation}
Projection gives
\begin{equation}
\Mmat_r\dot\aT+\Kmat_r\aT
=\qem_r(\aT;\mupar)+\bm f_b(\mupar),
\label{eq:rom_general}
\end{equation}
where
\[
\Mmat_r=\PhiT_r^T\Mmat\PhiT_r,
\qquad
\Kmat_r=\PhiT_r^T\Kmat\PhiT_r,
\qquad
\qem_r=\PhiT_r^T\bm Q_{\mathrm{em}}.
\]
With the mass-orthonormal spectral basis, \(\Mmat_r=\I\) and \(\Kmat_r=\Lam\). After absorbing fixed boundary forcing into \(\gem\),
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\dot\aT+\Lam(\mupar)\aT
=\gem(\aT;\mupar).
}
\label{eq:reduced_dynamics}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Reduced rank selection}
The retained dimension is not prescribed. Let \(V_r\subset V_{r_c}\subset V_h\) be nested thermal spaces. The smallest \(r\) is selected such that the final quantity-of-interest certificate is satisfied. If the cut occurs inside an inseparable spectral cluster, the entire cluster is retained.

\section{Deterministic temperature-dependent electromagnetic operator}

\subsection{Reduced electromagnetic feedback map}
For \(\aT\), reconstruct only the temperature information required by the electromagnetic backend,
\[
T_r=T_b+\PhiT_r\aT.
\]
The backend computes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_r(\aT;\mupar)
=\left\{
\mathbf Z(\aT),
P_{\mathrm{Cu}}(\aT),
P_{\mathrm{sea}}(\aT),
\qem_r(\aT)
\right\}.
\label{eq:em_reduced_map}
\end{equation}
The network never replaces this operator during training.

Geometry-only electromagnetic integrals are cached. Temperature-dependent material terms are updated through their physical constitutive laws. Any operator compression must provide an independent error estimator.

\subsection{Differentiability and Jacobian}

\begin{assumption}[Electromagnetic regularity]
On a compact certified thermal/parameter set \(\mathcal D\), the constitutive laws are continuously differentiable, \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{em}}(\aT;\mupar)\) is uniformly invertible, and all required operator derivatives are bounded.
\end{assumption}

Differentiate \eqref{eq:em_operator}. If \(\bm b_{\mathrm{em}}\) is temperature independent,
\begin{equation}
D_{\aT}\bm u_{\mathrm{em}}[\bm v]
=-\mathcal A_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1}
\left(D_{\aT}\mathcal A_{\mathrm{em}}[\bm v]\right)
\bm u_{\mathrm{em}}.
\label{eq:em_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
If the excitation also depends on temperature, add
\(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1}D_{\aT}\bm b_{\mathrm{em}}[\bm v]\).
By differentiating the loss functional and projection, one obtains
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_{\mathrm{em}}(\aT;\mupar)
=\frac{\partial\gem}{\partial\aT}.
\label{eq:Jem}
\end{equation}
This Jacobian is used for stability certification and residual-directed enrichment. It is not a learned quantity.

\section{Analytic neural network: neurons as closed-form dynamical responses}

\subsection{Basic philosophy}
The neural network does not use conventional time-stepping recurrent cells and does not use a black-box activation to approximate time. Each dynamic neuron is itself the exact solution operator of a first-order dissipative equation whose decay rate is taken from the physical thermal spectrum.

\subsection{Static parameter nodes}
Each geometry, operating, or initial-state coordinate may appear as a zero-dynamics node
\begin{equation}
\dot p_j=0,
\qquad
p_j(t)=p_j(0).
\end{equation}
Thus parameter dependence is part of the same analytic computation graph rather than an external encoder.

\subsection{Base thermal nodes}
The homogeneous response of thermal mode \(i\) is
\begin{equation}
h_i^{(0)}(t)=a_{0,i}e^{-\lambda_i t}.
\label{eq:base_node}
\end{equation}
These nodes enforce the exact initial state before any nonlinear correction is added.

\subsection{Analytic response neuron}

\begin{definition}[Analytic response neuron]
For a target physical decay rate \(\lambda_i(\mupar)>0\), an analytic response neuron is defined by
\begin{equation}
\left(\partial_t+\lambda_i\right)h_\nu(t)
=\psi_\nu(t;\mupar,\aT_0),
\qquad
h_\nu(0)=0,
\label{eq:neuron_ode}
\end{equation}
where \(\psi_\nu\) is formed from previously available nodes using finite additions, scalar multiplications, and products. Its forward operation is the exact closed-form response
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
h_\nu(t)=\int_0^t e^{-\lambda_i(t-\tau)}\psi_\nu(\tau)\,d\tau.
}
\label{eq:neuron_flow}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The network is a directed acyclic graph of static parameter nodes, base thermal nodes, multiplicative interaction nodes, analytic response neurons, and a final linear physical-state aggregation. The trainable quantities are connection coefficients in the source expressions and output aggregation; the temporal propagation law of each neuron is fixed by physics.

\section{Analytic closure of the network}

\subsection{Analytic atom algebra}
For a thermal spectrum \(\boldsymbol\lambda=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)\), define the additive spectral semigroup
\[
\mathcal S_\Lambda
=\left\{
\rho=\bm n^T\boldsymbol\lambda:
\bm n\in\N_0^r
\right\}.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}
\mathscr A_\Lambda
=\spanop\left\{
t^m e^{-\rho t}:
\rho\in\mathcal S_\Lambda,\ m\in\N_0
\right\}.
\label{eq:analytic_algebra}
\end{equation}
Coefficients may be finite polynomials in static parameter and initial-state nodes.

\begin{lemma}[Algebraic closure]
If \(f,g\in\mathscr A_\Lambda\), then
\[
f+g\in\mathscr A_\Lambda,
\qquad
fg\in\mathscr A_\Lambda,
\qquad
\partial_t f\in\mathscr A_\Lambda.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Addition is immediate. Products satisfy
\[
(t^m e^{-\rho t})(t^n e^{-\sigma t})
=t^{m+n}e^{-(\rho+\sigma)t},
\]
and \(\rho+\sigma\in\mathcal S_\Lambda\). Differentiation yields a finite linear combination of \(t^{m-1}e^{-\rho t}\) and \(t^m e^{-\rho t}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closure under analytic response neurons]
For \(f\in\mathscr A_\Lambda\) and \(\lambda_i>0\), the zero-initial-condition solution of
\[
(\partial_t+\lambda_i)h=f
\]
belongs to \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
It is sufficient to consider \(f=t^m e^{-\rho t}\). Repeated differentiation with respect to \(\rho\) reduces the result to the convolution of exponentials. For \(m=0\),
\[
\int_0^t e^{-\lambda_i(t-\tau)}e^{-\rho\tau}d\tau
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{e^{-\rho t}-e^{-\lambda_i t}}{\lambda_i-\rho},&\rho\ne\lambda_i,\\[2mm]
te^{-\lambda_i t},&\rho=\lambda_i.
\end{cases}
\]
Both forms lie in \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\). Higher \(m\) follows by differentiation with respect to \(\rho\), producing only polynomial factors in \(t\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Resonance-safe unified evaluation}
The apparent division by \(\lambda_i-\rho\) is not treated by an empirical branch. Define
\[
\varphi_1(z)=\frac{e^z-1}{z},\qquad\varphi_1(0)=1.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\int_0^t e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}e^{-\rho\tau}d\tau
=t e^{-\lambda t}\varphi_1\big((\lambda-\rho)t\big),
\label{eq:phi1}
\end{equation}
which is analytic at resonance. Higher polynomial-exponential atoms use the standard higher \(\varphi_k\) functions or their recurrence, again without heuristic switching.

\begin{theorem}[Analytic-network closure]
Every output of any finite analytic-response network constructed from the allowed nodes belongs to \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\) with parameter-polynomial coefficients. Hence the network output, its time derivative, and all intermediate dynamic nodes are available in closed analytic form.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Base nodes belong to \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\). Static parameter nodes are time constants. The result follows by induction over the directed acyclic graph using the two closure lemmas.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact initial condition]
If the output is assembled as
\[
\aT_N(t)=\aT_{\mathrm{hom}}(t)+\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal C}\bm c_\nu h_\nu(t),
\]
where \(\aT_{\mathrm{hom},i}=a_{0,i}e^{-\lambda_i t}\) and every correction neuron has zero initial condition, then
\[
\aT_N(0)=\aT_0
\]
exactly for every parameter and every network weight.
\end{corollary}

\section{Approximation capability}

\subsection{Time compactification from a physical eigenvalue}
Let \(\lambda_1(\mupar)>0\) be the first retained thermal decay rate and define
\begin{equation}
x(\mupar,t)=e^{-\lambda_1(\mupar)t}.
\end{equation}
On a compact parameter domain with \(\lambda_1\) continuous and uniformly positive, the map
\[
(\mupar,t)\mapsto(\mupar,x)
\]
is a homeomorphism between the compactified time domain \(\mathcal P\times[0,\infty]\) and \(\mathcal P\times[0,1]\), where \(t=\infty\) maps to \(x=0\).

\begin{assumption}[Continuous stable solution map]
On the certified domain, the reduced physical problem \eqref{eq:reduced_dynamics} has a unique solution map
\[
\mathcal S:(\mupar,\aT_0,t)\mapsto\aT(t)
\]
that is continuous on a compact set \(\mathcal P\times\mathcal A_0\times[0,\infty]\) and has a finite long-time limit.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform approximation on the certified compact domain]
Let \(\mathcal N\) be the union of analytic-response networks whose source dictionary contains constants, parameter coordinates, initial-state coordinates, products of existing nodes, and response neurons driven by retained physical decay rates. Then for every \(\varepsilon>0\) there exists a finite network \(N\in\mathcal N\) such that
\[
\sup_{\mupar\in\mathcal P,\,\aT_0\in\mathcal A_0,\,t\in[0,\infty]}
\norm{\mathcal S(\mupar,\aT_0,t)-N(\mupar,\aT_0,t)}
<\varepsilon.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Under the time compactification, the target becomes a continuous function of \((\mupar,\aT_0,x)\) on a compact set. The algebra generated by constants, parameter coordinates, initial-state coordinates, and \(x\) separates points and is closed under multiplication. By Stone--Weierstrass, its finite polynomials are uniformly dense in the continuous functions on that compact set. The network can generate \(x^n=e^{-n\lambda_1 t}\) through products of a physical base exponential and can generate all required parameter and initial-state monomials through static nodes and multiplication. Linear aggregation gives the approximating polynomial. Analytic response neurons enlarge, rather than restrict, this algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact anchoring at \(t=0\)]
If exact initial-condition preservation is required during approximation, apply the theorem to the correction. For a continuously differentiable physical solution, write
\[
\mathcal S(\mupar,\aT_0,t)-\aT_0
=(1-x)\,H(\mupar,\aT_0,x),
\]
with continuous \(H\) under the standard initial compatibility condition. Approximate \(H\) by the network algebra; the factor \(1-x\) is itself generated analytically. The approximation then satisfies \(N(0)=\aT_0\) exactly.
\end{remark}

\section{Physical residual and solution-data-free training}

\subsection{Exact reduced residual}
For network output \(\aT_N\), define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Res_N(t;\mupar,\aT_0)
=\dot\aT_N+\Lam\aT_N-\gem(\aT_N;\mupar).
}
\label{eq:nn_residual}
\end{equation}
Because \(\aT_N\) is analytic in time, \(\dot\aT_N\) is evaluated analytically. No numerical time integration and no time automatic differentiation are required.

The canonical neural objective is the squared dual/energy residual
\begin{equation}
\mathcal J_N
=\int_{\mathcal P}\int_{\mathcal A_0}\int_{0}^{\infty}
\norm{\Res_N(t;\mupar,\aT_0)}_{\mathcal R^{-1}}^2
\,d\nu_t\,d\nu_0\,d\nu_\mu,
\label{eq:training_functional}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal R\) is the Riesz map induced by the thermal energy metric and the measures define the stated certified operating domain. The correctness criterion is the physical residual itself; no labelled trajectory is present.

Numerical evaluation of \eqref{eq:training_functional} may use an adaptive integration scheme, but final acceptance uses independent certification. Integration error is required to be below the portion of the final output error bound assigned to numerical evaluation; arbitrary sampling density is not a correctness assumption.

\section{Residual-driven analytic network growth}

\subsection{Nested analytic trial graphs}
Let
\[
\mathcal V_0\subset\mathcal V_1\subset\cdots
\]
be nested network trial classes, where \(\mathcal V_{N+1}\) contains every function representable by \(\mathcal V_N\) plus one admissible analytic neuron and its outgoing linear connection. The initial class contains the homogeneous thermal nodes and constant-forcing response nodes required by the physical problem.

\subsection{Candidate selection from the unresolved residual}
At the current network \(\aT_N\), let \(\mathcal L_N\) denote the Frechet derivative of the residual operator,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_N[\delta\aT]
=\partial_t\delta\aT+\Lam\delta\aT
-\mathbf J_{\mathrm{em}}(\aT_N;\mupar)\delta\aT.
\label{eq:linearized_residual}
\end{equation}
For a normalized candidate correction \(\psi_\nu\), define the predicted residual-reduction score
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I_\nu
=
\frac{
\left|
\ip{\Res_N}{\mathcal L_N[\psi_\nu]}
\right|^2
}{
\norm{\mathcal L_N[\psi_\nu]}^2
}.
\label{eq:greedy_score}
\end{equation}
The next neuron is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\nu^*=\argmax_{\nu\in\mathcal D_N}\mathcal I_\nu,
}
\label{eq:greedy_select}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal D_N\) is the next complete level of the analytic interaction dictionary. This selects the direction giving the largest first-order reducible component of the unresolved physical residual.

\subsection{Non-empirical depth and width}
After enrichment, all weights in the enlarged graph are re-optimized with respect to the physical residual. Because \(\mathcal V_N\subset\mathcal V_{N+1}\), the previous solution remains feasible and therefore the globally minimized residual cannot increase.

\begin{theorem}[Residual convergence under complete enrichment]
Assume: (i) the union of the nested analytic trial classes is dense in the target solution class; (ii) the physical residual is coercive with respect to the state error on the certified domain; (iii) each enriched optimization returns a residual no larger than the preceding optimum; and (iv) the enrichment procedure is complete, meaning that no residual-detectable direction is permanently excluded. Then
\[
\inf_{v\in\mathcal V_N}\norm{\mathcal R(v)}\longrightarrow0
\qquad\text{as }N\to\infty.
\]
If the greedy candidate search realizes the maximal linearized residual reduction in each complete dictionary level, the adaptive sequence reaches the same zero-residual closure limit whenever the nonlinear re-optimization remains in the certified coercive branch.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Density gives a sequence in the union of trial classes converging to the physical solution. Coercivity and continuity of the residual imply that the best attainable residual over the nested classes tends to zero. Nestedness gives monotonic non-increase of the globally minimized residual. Completeness of enrichment prevents stagnation at a nonzero residual that has a nonzero component along an admissible correction direction. The final statement follows by combining the greedy linearized selection with nonlinear re-optimization inside the enlarged nested class.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The theorem deliberately states the conditions required for convergence. A generic nonconvex optimizer does not by itself prove global convergence. In implementation, optimization error is included as a numerical residual contribution and final physical certification is independent of the training optimizer.
\end{remark}

\section{Well-posedness, dissipativity, and long-time certification}

Let
\begin{equation}
\bm F(\aT;\mupar)
=-\Lam\aT+\gem(\aT;\mupar).
\end{equation}

\subsection{A computable contraction margin}
Define
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\mathrm{em}}
=
\sup_{(\aT,\mupar)\in\mathcal D}
\lambda_{\max}\left(
\sym\mathbf J_{\mathrm{em}}(\aT;\mupar)
\right),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa
=\inf_{\mupar\in\mathcal P}\lambda_{\min}(\Lam(\mupar))
-\beta_{\mathrm{em}}.
}
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Strong dissipativity on the certified domain]
If \(\kappa>0\), then for any \(\aT,\bm b\in\mathcal D\),
\[
\ip{\bm F(\aT)-\bm F(\bm b)}{\aT-\bm b}
\le-\kappa\norm{\aT-\bm b}^2.
\]
Hence the reduced flow is contractive. If an equilibrium exists in the invariant physical domain, it is unique and all trajectories in that domain converge to it exponentially.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the mean-value representation
\[
\gem(\aT)-\gem(\bm b)
=\int_0^1\mathbf J_{\mathrm{em}}(\bm b+s(\aT-\bm b))(\aT-\bm b)\,ds.
\]
The symmetric Jacobian bound gives
\[
\ip{\gem(\aT)-\gem(\bm b)}{\aT-\bm b}
\le\beta_{\mathrm{em}}\norm{\aT-\bm b}^2.
\]
The thermal term contributes at most
\(-\lambda_{\min}(\Lam)\norm{\aT-\bm b}^2\). Combining them yields the result.
\end{proof}

If \(\kappa\le0\), SDF-MPNEO does not claim infinite-time contraction. A finite-time Lipschitz certificate is used, or the operating point is declared outside the long-time certified domain. The architecture is never forced to be stable when the physical model itself is not certified stable.

\section{A posteriori error certification}

\subsection{Error decomposition}
Let \(T_h\) denote the selected high-fidelity spatial-discrete thermal solution, \(T_r\) the exact solution of the retained reduced model using the exact deterministic electromagnetic map, and \(T_N\) the analytic-network approximation evaluated with the numerical electromagnetic backend. Decompose conceptually
\[
T_h-T_N
=(T_h-T_r)+(T_r-T_N).
\]
The total computable residual is decomposed into
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\mathrm{tot}}
\le
\eta_{\mathrm{ROM}}
+\eta_{\mathrm{NN}}
+\eta_{\mathrm{EM}}
+\eta_{\mathrm{num}},
\label{eq:eta_total}
\end{equation}
where the terms denote spatial truncation, analytic-network residual, electromagnetic numerical error, and residual/certification numerical evaluation error.

\subsection{Independent higher-space residual}
Let \(V_r\subset V_{r_c}\) with \(r_c>r\). The network is constructed in \(V_r\), while the certifier tests the lifted temperature in \(V_{r_c}\):
\begin{equation}
\Res_c(v;t)
=m(\partial_t T_N,v)+k(T_N,v)-\ell_{\mathrm{em}}(T_N;v)
\qquad \forall v\in V_{r_c}.
\label{eq:high_residual}
\end{equation}
The certification space is enlarged until the spectral tail bound associated with the final output requirement is satisfied. Thus the network is not allowed to certify itself only in its training space.

\subsection{State-error bound in a contractive domain}
Let \(\widetilde\aT\) be the analytic-network state and \(\aT\) the exact reduced state. Define the exact-equation residual
\[
\bm r(t)=\dot{\widetilde\aT}-\bm F(\widetilde\aT).
\]
Because the initial condition is exact, \(\widetilde\aT(0)=\aT(0)\).

\begin{theorem}[Residual-to-state a posteriori bound]
If \(\kappa>0\), then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\norm{\widetilde\aT(t)-\aT(t)}
\le
\int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t-\tau)}\norm{\bm r(\tau)}\,d\tau.
}
\label{eq:state_bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\norm{\widetilde\aT(t)-\aT(t)}
\le
\frac{1-e^{-\kappa t}}{\kappa}
\norm{\bm r}_{L^\infty(0,t)}
\le
\frac{\norm{\bm r}_{L^\infty}}{\kappa}.
}
\label{eq:uniform_state_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\bm e=\widetilde\aT-\aT\). Then
\[
\dot{\bm e}=\bm F(\widetilde\aT)-\bm F(\aT)+\bm r.
\]
Using strong dissipativity,
\[
\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\norm{\bm e}^2
\le-\kappa\norm{\bm e}^2+\norm{\bm e}\norm{\bm r}.
\]
For nonzero \(\bm e\), divide by \(\norm{\bm e}\) and integrate the scalar differential inequality with \(\bm e(0)=0\).
\end{proof}

For the full model, the norm of \(\bm r\) in \eqref{eq:state_bound} is replaced by a certified bound containing the terms in \eqref{eq:eta_total}. Therefore long-time error does not grow without bound when \(\kappa>0\).

\subsection{Finite-time certificate outside the contractive domain}
If only a Lipschitz constant \(L\) is certified, then
\begin{equation}
\norm{\bm e(t)}
\le
\int_0^t e^{L(t-\tau)}\norm{\bm r(\tau)}d\tau.
\label{eq:finite_time_bound}
\end{equation}
Such a case is explicitly marked as finite-horizon certified rather than long-time certified.

\section{Electromagnetic numerical error and output certification}

\subsection{Required backend certificate}
The electromagnetic backend must return, in addition to nominal quantities,
\begin{align}
\norm{\delta\qem_r}&\le\eta_{\mathrm{EM},q},\\
|\delta Z_{ij}|&\le\eta_{\mathrm{EM},Z_{ij}},
\end{align}
with analogous bounds for power quantities. These bounds may be assembled from certified quadrature remainder, operator-compression error, and linear-solve residual/error bounds. The theory does not assume that an unchecked numerical electromagnetic value is exact.

\subsection{Impedance error}
Let \(Z(\aT;\mupar)\) be differentiable on the certified thermal set and define
\[
L_Z=\sup_{(\aT,\mupar)\in\mathcal D}
\norm{D_{\aT}Z(\aT;\mupar)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|Z(\aT)-\widetilde Z(\widetilde\aT)|
\le
L_Z\norm{\aT-\widetilde\aT}
+\eta_{\mathrm{EM},Z}.
}
\label{eq:Z_bound}
\end{equation}
The same construction applies to \(R,L,M,P_{\mathrm{Cu}},P_{\mathrm{sea}}\).

\subsection{Maximum-temperature error}
For a finite-dimensional certified spatial space with basis vector \(\bm\varphi(\bm x)\), define the computable embedding constant
\[
C_\infty
=\sup_{\bm x\in\Omega}
\norm{\Mmat^{-1/2}\bm\varphi(\bm x)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\norm{T_h-T_N}_{L^\infty}
\le C_\infty\norm{T_h-T_N}_{\Mmat},
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
|T_{\max,h}-T_{\max,N}|
\le C_\infty\norm{T_h-T_N}_{\Mmat}.
}
\label{eq:Tmax_bound}
\end{equation}

\section{Unified non-empirical model selection}

The user or application specifies physically meaningful final tolerances, for example
\[
|T_{\max}-T_{\max,N}|\le\varepsilon_T,
\qquad
\frac{|Z-Z_N|}{|Z|}\le\varepsilon_Z.
\]
The method then chooses thermal dimension \(r\), analytic-neuron count/topology \(N\), and electromagnetic numerical accuracy level \(p_{\mathrm{em}}\) by solving
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\min_{r,N,p_{\mathrm{em}}}
C_{\mathrm{online}}(r,N,p_{\mathrm{em}})
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\mathcal B_Q(r,N,p_{\mathrm{em}})\le\varepsilon_Q
\ \forall Q\in\mathcal Q.
}
\label{eq:model_selection}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal B_Q\) is the certified quantity-of-interest error bound assembled from spatial, neural, electromagnetic, and numerical residual contributions. Internal tolerances are consequences of \eqref{eq:model_selection}; they are not manually assigned error budgets.

\section{Training algorithm}

\subsection{Offline physics construction}
For the certified parameter domain:
\begin{enumerate}
\item construct the unified reference-domain geometry maps;
\item assemble thermal mass/stiffness operators and isolated spectral clusters;
\item build canonical thermal coordinates using spectral projectors and anchor charts;
\item establish an initial reduced thermal space and an independent higher certification space;
\item initialize the deterministic electromagnetic temperature-parameter interface, projected loss source, Jacobian action, and numerical error estimator.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Solution-data-free analytic-network training}
For each current analytic trial graph:
\begin{enumerate}
\item evaluate \(\aT_N(\mupar,\aT_0,t)\) in closed form;
\item evaluate \(\dot\aT_N\) analytically;
\item call the deterministic electromagnetic operator at \(\aT_N\) to obtain \(\gem\) and required Jacobian actions;
\item compute the physical residual \eqref{eq:nn_residual};
\item optimize network connection coefficients to minimize the physical residual functional;
\item compute independent higher-space and output certificates;
\item if the certificate fails because of thermal spatial error, enlarge the thermal spectral space;
\item if it fails because of analytic-network residual, select the next neuron by \eqref{eq:greedy_select};
\item if it fails because of electromagnetic numerical error, tighten the certified electromagnetic operator accuracy;
\item stop only when all requested output bounds are satisfied.
\end{enumerate}
No transient high-fidelity solution is generated or consumed by this procedure.

\section{Online inference}

For a new certified sample \((\mupar,T_0,t_q)\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item map the geometry to the appropriate canonical thermal coordinate chart;
\item project \(T_0\) deterministically to obtain \(\aT_0\);
\item evaluate the analytic neural graph directly at \(t_q\) to obtain \(\aT_N(t_q)\); no time stepping is performed;
\item reconstruct only the temperature quantities required by the user or electromagnetic backend;
\item call the deterministic electromagnetic operator once at \(\aT_N(t_q)\) to obtain \(\mathbf Z,R,L,M,P_{\mathrm{Cu}},P_{\mathrm{sea}}\);
\item optionally evaluate the online certificate.
\end{enumerate}
The cost of querying \(t_q=1\,\mathrm s\) or \(t_q=10^4\,\mathrm s\) depends on analytic graph size, not on the elapsed physical time.

\section{Main theoretical results and dependency chain}

The framework is logically closed by the following results.

\begin{theorem}[T1: parameter-consistent spatial coordinates]
Under uniform geometric regularity, thermal coercivity, isolated retained spectral clusters, and nonsingular canonical anchors, the reduced thermal subspace and its canonical coordinate representation vary continuously with geometry and contain whole eigenvalue-crossing clusters.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[T2: analytic neural closure]
Every finite analytic-response network has a closed-form output in the polynomial-exponential algebra \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\), with exact analytic time derivatives and exact initial-state anchoring.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[T3: approximation completeness]
On a compact certified parameter/initial-state domain with continuous stable solution map, the union of analytic-response networks is uniformly dense in the transient solution map, including the compactified infinite-time limit.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[T4: adaptive-growth convergence under certified assumptions]
If the nested analytic trial classes are dense, the physical residual is coercive with respect to state error, optimization does not increase the residual, and the enrichment dictionary is complete, then the best residual over the adaptively enlarged analytic-network classes converges to zero. Residual-greedy selection supplies a physics-directed sequence of enrichments.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[T5: unified a posteriori certification]
If the coupled reduced dynamics are strongly dissipative with margin \(\kappa>0\), then the total thermal-state error is bounded uniformly in time by the certified total residual divided by \(\kappa\). Electromagnetic and maximum-temperature output bounds follow by sensitivity and embedding estimates such as \eqref{eq:Z_bound} and \eqref{eq:Tmax_bound}.
\end{theorem}

These results have a strict dependency order:
\[
\boxed{
\text{geometry regularity}
\Rightarrow
\text{consistent physical coordinates}
\Rightarrow
\text{reduced dynamics}
\Rightarrow
\text{analytic network}
\Rightarrow
\text{residual convergence}
\Rightarrow
\text{certified outputs}.
}
\]

\section{Remaining implementation proof obligations}

The framework above separates mathematical structure from backend-specific quantities that must be computed or bounded in implementation. The following are not free empirical choices; they are required proof/certification tasks.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Geometry-map certificate:} verify Jacobian nondegeneracy and material-subdomain correspondence over the chosen parameter domain.
\item \textbf{Spectral-cluster certificate:} bound the retained/discarded spectral gap and canonical-anchor rank; create a new deterministic chart if the current anchor becomes singular.
\item \textbf{Thermal-tail certificate:} compute a posteriori residual/spectral-tail bounds sufficient for the requested output tolerance.
\item \textbf{Electromagnetic regularity certificate:} verify uniform solvability of the temperature-parameter electromagnetic operator over the certified thermal range.
\item \textbf{Electromagnetic derivative interface:} implement \(D_{\aT}\gem\) or certified Jacobian-vector products from the deterministic backend.
\item \textbf{Dissipativity certificate:} compute or rigorously bound \(\beta_{\mathrm{em}}\) and hence \(\kappa\). If \(\kappa\le0\), switch to finite-time certification rather than asserting long-time stability.
\item \textbf{Electromagnetic numerical error certificate:} expose bounds for projected source and output errors from quadrature, compression, and linear solves.
\item \textbf{Independent final validation:} high-fidelity transient simulations and experiments may be used only after training to test the theory and measure sharpness of certificates; they do not become training labels.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Final framework definition}

The final SDF-MPNEO architecture is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{unified deterministic geometry}\
+\\[-1mm]
\text{physics-derived thermal spectral reduction}\
+\\[-1mm]
\text{analytic-response neural dynamics}\
+\\[-1mm]
\text{deterministic temperature-dependent electromagnetic feedback}\
+\\[-1mm]
\text{residual-driven neural topology growth}\
+\\[-1mm]
\text{unified a posteriori certification}.
\end{array}
}
\]
The defining distinction is that the neural computation unit is not a conventional scalar activation. A neuron is an exactly solvable local dynamical response operator. The physical thermal spectrum supplies its temporal generator; multiplicative neural connections generate nonlinear cross-mode interactions; the deterministic electromagnetic operator supplies the actual nonlinear thermal source during training; and unresolved physical residual determines which analytic interaction is added next.

In compact form, the exact reduced physics are
\[
\dot\aT+\Lam\aT=\gem(\aT;\mupar),
\]
the analytic network provides
\[
\aT_N(t)=\mathcal N_{\mathrm{analytic}}(\mupar,\aT_0,t),
\]
training enforces
\[
\dot\aT_N+\Lam\aT_N-\gem(\aT_N;\mupar)\rightarrow0,
\]
and certification converts the remaining physical residual into explicit error bounds for temperature and electromagnetic outputs.

\section{Theoretical claim discipline}

The following statements are part of the framework and may be claimed when their assumptions are verified:
\begin{itemize}
\item the network output is analytically evaluable in time;
\item the initial condition is exact by construction;
\item no high-fidelity solution label is required for training;
\item network topology can be selected by unresolved physical residual rather than a prescribed width/depth;
\item arbitrary query time does not require temporal marching;
\item in a certified contractive domain, residual error yields a uniform-in-time state error bound.
\end{itemize}

The following must \emph{not} be claimed without additional proof or computation:
\begin{itemize}
\item global long-time stability outside the domain where \(\kappa>0\) is certified;
\item zero spatial-reduction error at finite \(r\);
\item exact electromagnetic output when the deterministic electromagnetic backend has nonzero numerical error;
\item global convergence of a particular nonconvex training optimizer;
\item uniqueness of a canonical spectral chart when its anchor overlap is singular;
\item novelty over all prior literature without a separate literature and patent search.
\end{itemize}
