\begin{abstract}
This document fixes the theoretical baseline of SDF-MPNEO for underwater wireless power transfer. The method is derived directly from the spatial magnetoquasistatic electromagnetic equations and the conjugate heat equation. It contains no geometry-specific impedance solver as a core dependency. Electromagnetic and thermal spaces are reduced deterministically without full-solution snapshots. Geometry variation and temperature-dependent copper/seawater conductivity are included in a common certified parameter domain. The electromagnetic state is quasi-statically eliminated from the slower thermal dynamics, and copper/seawater Joule losses are projected directly to reduced thermal coordinates. Time evolution is represented by an analytic neural network whose neurons are themselves closed-form dissipative response operators. The network topology grows from unresolved physical residuals. Electromagnetic reduction error, operator-separation error, thermal reduction error, analytic-network residual, and numerical solve error are propagated into one a posteriori output certificate. All free approximation orders are selected from rigorous output-error requirements rather than empirical rules.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and non-empirical rules}

The retained physics are
\[
\boxed{\text{magnetoquasistatic electromagnetics}\longleftrightarrow\text{heat transfer}}.
\]
The physical domain is partitioned into conductor, package/insulator, seawater, and optional passive subdomains,
\[
\Omega(\mupar)=\Omega_{\rm Cu}\cup\Omega_{\rm p}\cup\Omega_{\rm sea}\cup\Omega_{\rm other}.
\]
Fluid velocity is outside the current model scope; seawater remains an electromagnetic and thermal spatial medium.

The parameter vector
\[
\mupar=(\mu_g,\mu_u,\mu_m)\in\mathcal P
\]
contains geometry, fixed operating conditions, and material parameters. The online map is
\[
(\mupar,T_0,t_q)\mapsto
\{T(\mathbf x,t_q),\mathbf Z(t_q),R,L,M,P_{\rm Cu},P_{\rm sea},\mathcal C\},
\]
where \(\mathcal C\) denotes the numerical/physical certificate and no time marching from \(0\) to \(t_q\) is permitted online.

\paragraph{Method rules.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(R\arabic*)]
\item No labelled full-order electromagnetic, thermal, or experimental solution is used to train the analytic evolution network.
\item No full-order solution snapshots are required to construct the reduced electromagnetic or thermal spaces.
\item No geometry-specific impedance solver is a core dependency.
\item No empirical near/far split, fixed reduced rank, fixed neural depth/width, artificial time constant, or fitted correction coefficient defines the method.
\item Copper skin/proximity effects and seawater conductive reaction arise from the spatial electromagnetic field equations.
\item Temperature enters electromagnetics through constitutive laws such as \(\sigma_{\rm Cu}(T)\) and \(\sigma_{\rm sea}(T)\), not through empirical impedance corrections.
\item Non-affine operator compression is accepted only with a deterministic remainder bound entering the final certificate.
\item A parameter point outside the certified stability/geometry/material domain is reported as non-certified rather than silently extrapolated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Full coupled field equations}

\subsection{Magnetoquasistatic electromagnetic problem}
For harmonic angular frequency \(\omega\), define magnetic vector potential \(\mathbf A\), electric scalar potential \(\phi\),
\[
\mathbf B=\nabla\times\mathbf A,
\qquad
\mathbf E=-j\omega\mathbf A-\nabla\phi,
\qquad
\mathbf J=\sigma(T)\mathbf E+\mathbf J_s.
\]
The magnetoquasistatic equations are
\begin{align}
\nabla\times\bigl(\nu(T)\nabla\times\mathbf A\bigr)
+\sigma(T)(j\omega\mathbf A+\nabla\phi)&=\mathbf J_s,
\label{eq:mqsa}\\
\nabla\cdot\{\sigma(T)(j\omega\mathbf A+\nabla\phi)\}&=0,
\label{eq:mqsphi}
\end{align}
with a compatible gauge and port/excitation constraints. In insulating subdomains \(\sigma=0\). In copper and seawater,
\[
\sigma_{\rm Cu}=\sigma_{\rm Cu}(T),
\qquad
\sigma_{\rm sea}=\sigma_{\rm sea}(T).
\]
Resolving the conductor cross-section in \eqref{eq:mqsa}--\eqref{eq:mqsphi} automatically includes current redistribution, skin effect, and proximity effect; no separate AC-resistance correction is required.

A compatible edge/nodal discretization gives a gauge-stabilized complex linear system
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Aem(\etaPar)\mathbf u=\bem(\mupar),
}
\label{eq:em_full_discrete}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf u\) contains discrete \(\mathbf A\), \(\phi\), and required constraint variables, and
\[
\etaPar=(\mupar,\aT)
\]
includes the thermal reduced coordinates because the conductivity field depends on temperature.

\subsection{Joule loss and electromagnetic outputs}
Using RMS phasors, local Joule heating is
\[
Q_J(\mathbf x)=\sigma(T)|\mathbf E(\mathbf x)|^2.
\]
Hence
\begin{align}
P_{\rm Cu}&=\int_{\Omega_{\rm Cu}}\sigma_{\rm Cu}(T)|\mathbf E|^2\,d\Omega,\\
P_{\rm sea}&=\int_{\Omega_{\rm sea}}\sigma_{\rm sea}(T)|\mathbf E|^2\,d\Omega.
\end{align}
Port voltage/current functionals of \(\mathbf u\) give the impedance matrix \(\mathbf Z\) and therefore \(R,L,M\). The exact prefactor is fixed once a single peak/RMS phasor convention is selected and is used consistently throughout the code.

\subsection{Thermal problem}
The temperature satisfies
\begin{equation}
\rho c_p\partial_tT-\nabla\cdot(k\nabla T)=Q_J(\mathbf u,T;\mupar)
\quad\text{in }\Omega(\mupar),
\label{eq:heat_full}
\end{equation}
with interface continuity of temperature and heat flux unless a physically specified contact resistance is present. Boundary conditions are physical inputs or are accompanied by an independently certified domain-truncation/open-boundary error.

\subsection{Multirate closure}
The electromagnetic relaxation time is assumed much smaller than the thermal time scale,
\[
\tau_{\rm em}\ll\tau_T.
\]
Therefore electromagnetics is quasi-steady at each instantaneous thermal state. This is a scale separation, not a decoupling: temperature modifies conductivity, which modifies electromagnetic fields and losses, which feed back into the heat equation.

\section{Unified reference domain and compatible coordinates}

Let \(F_{\mupar}:\widehat\Omega\to\Omega(\mupar)\) be a deterministic geometry map from a fixed reference domain.

\begin{assumption}[Uniform non-degeneracy]
For the certified geometry domain,
\[
0<J_-\le |\det DF_{\mupar}|\le J_+<\infty,
\qquad
\norm{DF_{\mupar}}+\norm{DF_{\mupar}^{-1}}\le C_F.
\]
\end{assumption}

Thermal scalar fields use the standard \(H^1\) pullback. Electromagnetic vector potentials use the covariant Piola transformation
\[
\mathbf A(\mathbf x)=DF_{\mupar}^{-T}\widehat{\mathbf A}(\widehat{\mathbf x}),
\]
which preserves the \(H(\mathrm{curl})\) structure and curl conformity. Consequently all geometries are represented in common reference finite-element spaces, and geometry dependence appears only in transformed coefficient tensors and boundary/port functionals.

This removes the need for geometry-specific solvers and makes one reduced electromagnetic space meaningful over a parameterized family of coil/package/seawater geometries, provided the map remains non-degenerate and topology does not change. Topology changes define a different certified chart/model.

\section{Deterministic thermal reduction}

After boundary lifting \(T=T_b+\widetilde T\), let the reference-domain thermal matrices be \(\Mt(\mupar)\) and \(\Kt(\mupar)\). Solve
\begin{equation}
\Kt(\mupar)\boldsymbol\phi_i
=\lambda_i(\mupar)\Mt(\mupar)\boldsymbol\phi_i,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\phi_i^T\Mt\boldsymbol\phi_j=\delta_{ij}.
\label{eq:thermal_eig}
\end{equation}
The selected spectral subspace is tracked by its Riesz projector rather than by individual eigenvectors, so eigenvalue crossings inside a retained cluster do not create coordinate permutations.

With a deterministic canonical basis \(\PhiT_r\),
\begin{equation}
T=T_b+\PhiT_r\aT,
\label{eq:thermal_rom_state}
\end{equation}
and after mass normalization
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\dot\aT+\LamT\aT=\mathbf g_{\rm em}(\aT;\mupar)+\mathbf f_T(\mupar).
}
\label{eq:thermal_closed_template}
\end{equation}
No reduced dimension is fixed a priori. The smallest spectral cluster satisfying the final output-error requirement is retained; if a truncation boundary intersects a multiple/near-crossing cluster, the entire cluster is retained.

\section{Solution-data-free electromagnetic reduction}

This section resolves the first major difficulty: construction of a fast electromagnetic reduced space without full-order solution snapshots.

\subsection{Common parameter domain}
Define
\[
\etaPar=(\mupar,\aT)\in\mathcal D_{\rm em}.
\]
The thermal coordinate set is not arbitrary. It is the certified reachable/allowable set implied by the initial-condition domain, material-temperature validity interval, and thermal energy bounds. Therefore geometry, excitation, load, frequency, copper temperature, and seawater temperature all enter one compact electromagnetic parameter domain.

\subsection{Riesz map and inf-sup stability}
Choose a fixed Hermitian positive-definite reference Riesz matrix \(\mathbf H\) on the gauge-stabilized electromagnetic state space. Define
\[
\norm{\mathbf v}_H^2=\mathbf v^*\mathbf H\mathbf v,
\qquad
\norm{\mathbf r}_{H^{-1}}^2=\mathbf r^*\mathbf H^{-1}\mathbf r.
\]
For \eqref{eq:em_full_discrete}, define the stability factor
\begin{equation}
\beta(\etaPar)=
\inf_{\mathbf v\ne0}
\frac{\norm{\Aem(\etaPar)\mathbf v}_{H^{-1}}}{\norm{\mathbf v}_H}.
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
The certified domain requires a computable lower bound
\[
\beta(\etaPar)\ge\beta_{\rm LB}(\etaPar)>0.
\]
A verified lower bound may be obtained from the minimum singular value of the scaled operator \(\mathbf H^{-1/2}\Aem\mathbf H^{-1/2}\), combined with the certified operator-separation representation introduced later. Global minimization over a compact parameter box is performed by deterministic interval/branch-and-bound bounds, not random sampling.

\subsection{Initial reduced space from operators and excitations}
Suppose the excitation family has a deterministic separated representation
\[
\bem(\mupar)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_b}\vartheta_q^b(\mupar)\mathbf b_q+\mathbf e_b,
\qquad
\norm{\mathbf e_b}_{H^{-1}}\le\varepsilon_b.
\]
Define
\begin{equation}
V_0=\operatorname{orth}_H\{\mathbf H^{-1}\mathbf b_1,\ldots,\mathbf H^{-1}\mathbf b_{Q_b},\text{required constraint/port modes}\}.
\label{eq:V0}
\end{equation}
This uses only physical operators, excitations, and constraints; no field solution snapshot appears.

\subsection{Minimum-residual reduced solve}
For an \(H\)-orthonormal basis \(V_n\), define
\begin{equation}
\mathbf c_n(\etaPar)=
\argmin_{\mathbf c}
\norm{\bem(\mupar)-\Aem(\etaPar)V_n\mathbf c}_{H^{-1}}.
\label{eq:minres_rom}
\end{equation}
The reduced electromagnetic field is \(\mathbf u_n=V_n\mathbf c_n\), with residual
\begin{equation}
\mathbf r_n(\etaPar)=\bem(\mupar)-\Aem(\etaPar)V_n\mathbf c_n.
\end{equation}
From \eqref{eq:beta},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\norm{\mathbf u-\mathbf u_n}_H
\le
\Delta_n^{\rm em}(\etaPar)
:=\frac{\norm{\mathbf r_n}_{H^{-1}}+\varepsilon_{\rm op}\norm{\mathbf u_n}_H+\varepsilon_b}{\beta_{\rm LB}(\etaPar)}.
}
\label{eq:em_error_bound}
\end{equation}
Here \(\varepsilon_{\rm op}\) is the certified operator-separation error defined later.

\subsection{Worst-residual Riesz enrichment}
Determine
\begin{equation}
\etaPar_n^*=\argmax_{\etaPar\in\mathcal D_{\rm em}}
\Delta_n^{\rm em}(\etaPar),
\label{eq:worst_eta}
\end{equation}
using global branch-and-bound with certified upper/lower bounds. Then compute only the Riesz lift of the residual,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf w_n=\mathbf H^{-1}\mathbf r_n(\etaPar_n^*),
\label{eq:riesz_lift}
\end{equation}
and enrich
\begin{equation}
V_{n+1}=\operatorname{orth}_H[V_n,\mathbf w_n].
\label{eq:em_enrich}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:riesz_lift} uses one solve with the fixed reference Riesz matrix, not the physical full-order electromagnetic operator at \(\etaPar_n^*\). Hence it is an operator-residual correction, not a labelled solution snapshot.

\begin{proposition}[Monotone electromagnetic residual]
Because \(V_n\subset V_{n+1}\) and \eqref{eq:minres_rom} is a minimum-residual problem,
\[
\norm{\mathbf r_{n+1}(\etaPar)}_{H^{-1}}
\le
\norm{\mathbf r_n(\etaPar)}_{H^{-1}}
\]
for every \(\etaPar\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The old minimizer remains feasible in the enlarged reduced space, so the minimum over the enlarged space cannot be larger.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform data-free electromagnetic convergence]
Assume: (i) \(\mathcal D_{\rm em}\) is compact; (ii) \(\beta_{\rm LB}\ge\beta_0>0\); (iii) \(\Aem(\etaPar)\) and \(\bem(\mupar)\) vary continuously; (iv) the closure of the operator-generated enrichment dictionary contains the solution manifold. If each enrichment solves \eqref{eq:worst_eta} to certified global accuracy and adds a nonzero Riesz residual direction, then
\[
\sup_{\etaPar\in\mathcal D_{\rm em}}\Delta_n^{\rm em}(\etaPar)\to0
\]
up to the explicitly retained operator/excitation remainder bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof structure]
Monotonicity gives a non-increasing worst residual. If its limit were positive, compactness and continuity would provide a parameter point with nonzero limiting residual. Density of the enrichment dictionary gives a state direction producing a strict reduction of the minimum residual, contradicting stagnation. Uniform inf-sup stability converts residual convergence to state convergence. Operator/excitation remainder terms form the nonzero certified floor and are reduced independently until the output target is met.
\end{proof}

This theorem resolves both the ``no snapshot basis'' problem and the ``temperature changes the electromagnetic operator'' problem: thermal coordinates are part of \(\etaPar\), so the same worst-case enrichment certifies the reduced electromagnetic space over the complete allowed temperature field family.

\section{Certified non-affine operator separation}

Fast online evaluation requires reduced matrices to be assembled without traversing the full spatial mesh. No empirical interpolation is required.

\subsection{Temperature-dependent constitutive laws}
Let the certified temperature interval be \([T_-,T_+]\). For a constitutive coefficient, e.g. \(\sigma_{\rm sea}(T)\), use either its exact algebraic form or a deterministic polynomial/Chebyshev approximation
\begin{equation}
\sigma(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{p}s_m\chi_m(T)+r_p(T),
\qquad
\sup_{T\in[T_-,T_+]}|r_p(T)|\le\varepsilon_\sigma(p).
\label{eq:sigma_cheb}
\end{equation}
The smallest \(p\) satisfying the propagated output-error budget is selected. The remainder bound follows from derivative/Bernstein-ellipse bounds for analytic laws or verified interval interpolation bounds for certified tabulated material data.

Since
\[
T=T_b+\PhiT_r\aT,
\]
\eqref{eq:sigma_cheb} becomes a finite tensor polynomial in \(\aT\) plus a certified remainder. The same construction is used for copper conductivity and any temperature-dependent permeability when physically required.

\subsection{Geometry dependence}
After the reference-domain pullback, Jacobian and metric tensors depend on geometry parameters. Exact rational/algebraic dependence is retained when available; otherwise deterministic multivariate Chebyshev approximation with interval-certified remainder is used. Consequently
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Aem(\etaPar)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q_A}\theta_q(\etaPar)\mathbf A_q+\mathbf E_A(\etaPar),
\qquad
\norm{\mathbf E_A}_{H\to H^{-1}}\le\varepsilon_{\rm op}.
}
\label{eq:affine_em}
\end{equation}
The reduced matrices \(V^*\mathbf A_qV\) are precomputed offline.

\subsection{Certified tensor compression}
If direct polynomial expansion produces many coefficient tensors, deterministic matrix/tensor SVD or hierarchical factorization may be used. Terms are discarded only if a rigorous spectral/Frobenius-to-operator norm bound proves their aggregate contribution is below the remaining operator-error budget. No weight-magnitude pruning rule defines the scientific method.

\section{Quasi-static electromagnetic elimination and reduced heat source}

For the final electromagnetic basis \(V_{\rm em}\), define
\begin{equation}
\mathbf A_{{\rm em},r}(\etaPar)=V_{\rm em}^*\Aem(\etaPar)V_{\rm em},
\qquad
\mathbf b_{{\rm em},r}=V_{\rm em}^*\bem.
\end{equation}
At a thermal state \(\aT\), solve only
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathbf A_{{\rm em},r}(\mupar,\aT)\cE
=\mathbf b_{{\rm em},r}(\mupar).
}
\label{eq:em_rom_solve}
\end{equation}
The electromagnetic coefficients are therefore an algebraic function
\[
\cE=\mathcal C_{\rm em}(\mupar,\aT).
\]
There is no electromagnetic time stepping inside the thermal evolution.

\subsection{Direct reduced loss projection}
Let \(D_E\) map electromagnetic state coefficients to discrete electric-field degrees of freedom. For the \(k\)-th thermal mode, define preassembled reduced quadratic forms
\begin{align}
\mathbf H_{k}^{\rm Cu}(\etaPar)
&=V_{\rm em}^*D_E^*\mathbf C_{\sigma\Phi_k}^{\rm Cu}(\etaPar)D_EV_{\rm em},\\
\mathbf H_{k}^{\rm sea}(\etaPar)
&=V_{\rm em}^*D_E^*\mathbf C_{\sigma\Phi_k}^{\rm sea}(\etaPar)D_EV_{\rm em}.
\end{align}
They use the same certified operator-separation machinery as \eqref{eq:affine_em}. The reduced thermal source is evaluated directly as
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
[q_{\rm em,r}]_k
=\cE^*\bigl(\mathbf H_k^{\rm Cu}+\mathbf H_k^{\rm sea}\bigr)\cE.
}
\label{eq:heat_tensor}
\end{equation}
Hence online evaluation does not reconstruct the full three-dimensional Joule-loss field. Copper and seawater losses remain spatially distinct because their coupling tensors are assembled on different physical subdomains.

The total powers are analogous quadratic forms,
\[
P_{\rm Cu}=\cE^*\mathbf H_P^{\rm Cu}\cE,
\qquad
P_{\rm sea}=\cE^*\mathbf H_P^{\rm sea}\cE.
\]
Port functionals evaluated on \(V_{\rm em}\cE\) provide \(\mathbf Z,R,L,M\).

\section{Closed reduced electromagnetic--thermal dynamics}

After electromagnetic elimination, define
\begin{equation}
\mathbf g_{\rm em}(\aT;\mupar)
=\Mt_r^{-1}\mathbf q_{\rm em,r}
\bigl(\mathcal C_{\rm em}(\mupar,\aT),\aT;\mupar\bigr).
\end{equation}
With mass-normalized thermal modes, the exact reduced closure is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\dot\aT+\LamT\aT
=\mathbf g_{\rm em}(\aT;\mupar)+\mathbf f_T(\mupar).
}
\label{eq:closed_et}
\end{equation}
All spatial copper loss, skin/proximity effect, seawater induced current, geometry dependence, and temperature feedback enter through \(\mathbf g_{\rm em}\). The analytic neural network approximates only the continuous-time solution map of \eqref{eq:closed_et}; it does not replace the electromagnetic or thermal operators.

\section{Analytic neural evolution network}

\subsection{Analytic function algebra}
For a fixed parameter point, let \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r>0\) be the thermal decay rates and define
\begin{equation}
\mathscr A_\Lambda=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{t^m e^{-\rho t}:m\in\mathbb N_0,\ \rho=\mathbf n^T\boldsymbol\lambda,\ \mathbf n\in\mathbb N_0^r\right\}.
\end{equation}
This set is closed under addition, multiplication, time differentiation, and the stable response operator \((\partial_t+\lambda_i)^{-1}\) with zero initial condition.

\subsection{Analytic neuron}
An analytic neuron is defined by
\begin{equation}
(\partial_t+\lambda_i)h_\nu(t)=S_\nu(t),
\qquad
h_\nu(0)=h_{\nu,0},
\label{eq:analytic_neuron}
\end{equation}
where the source \(S_\nu\) is an algebraic combination of static parameter nodes, initial-state nodes, and preceding analytic neurons. Its forward map is the closed form
\begin{equation}
h_\nu(t)=e^{-\lambda_i t}h_{\nu,0}
+\int_0^t e^{-\lambda_i(t-\tau)}S_\nu(\tau)\,d\tau.
\end{equation}
For an atom \(t^m e^{-\rho t}\), use the entire function
\[
\varphi_{m+1}(z)=\int_0^1 e^{(1-s)z}\frac{s^m}{m!}\,ds
\]
to evaluate resonant and non-resonant cases without an empirical denominator threshold. Thus each neuron, its time derivative, and any finite network composition remain explicitly analytic in time.

\begin{theorem}[Analytic closure]
If all source nodes belong to \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\), every finite analytic-neuron directed acyclic graph produces outputs in \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\). Its time derivative is available symbolically/recursively without numerical time differentiation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The base decay nodes belong to \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\). Closure under algebraic source construction and under \((\partial_t+\lambda_i)^{-1}\) yields the claim by induction over the directed acyclic graph.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Approximation capacity}
Choose any \(\lambda_*>0\) from the thermal spectrum and set \(x=e^{-\lambda_*t}\). The compactified time interval \(t\in[0,\infty]\) maps to \(x\in[0,1]\). For any continuous stable response component with a finite steady-state limit, polynomial approximation in \(x\) yields finite sums of \(e^{-n\lambda_*t}\), which are contained in \(\mathscr A_\Lambda\). With static parameter and initial-state nodes, the generated algebra separates points on a compact certified parameter/initial-state/time domain. Therefore the analytic network is dense in the continuous stable solution maps on that domain. This is an approximation statement; finite networks retain a certified residual rather than being asserted exact.

\section{Solution-data-free training and residual-grown topology}

The network returns \(\aT_\theta(t)\) and its exact analytic derivative. Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathbf R_\theta
=\dot\aT_\theta+\LamT\aT_\theta
-\mathbf g_{\rm em}(\aT_\theta;\mupar)-\mathbf f_T.
}
\label{eq:nn_residual}
\end{equation}
The primary training objective is the physical dual/energy norm of \(\mathbf R_\theta\), integrated over the certified parameter/initial-state/time domain with deterministic error-controlled quadrature. No transient field solution appears as a label.

Network size is not fixed. Let \(\mathcal D_N\) be the current analytic-neuron dictionary. Candidate sources are generated hierarchically from admissible products of active nodes and static parameter nodes. For candidate \(\psi\), define the Riesz residual correlation
\[
I(\psi)=\frac{|\ip{\mathcal R^{-1}\mathbf R_\theta}{\psi}|^2}{\norm{\psi}_{\mathcal R}^2}.
\]
Add the candidate with certified maximum correlation, re-optimize all active weights without increasing the physical residual, and repeat until the final output certificate is satisfied.

Under compactness, density of the analytic dictionary, and exact/controlled greedy selection, the monotone residual sequence converges to zero up to independent electromagnetic/thermal/operator-discretization error floors. Thus network topology is generated by unresolved physics rather than by empirical depth/width selection.

\section{Stability and unified error certification}

\subsection{Contractive domain}
For the exact closed reduced vector field
\[
F(\aT;\mupar)=-\LamT\aT+\mathbf g_{\rm em}(\aT;\mupar)+\mathbf f_T,
\]
define
\begin{equation}
\kappa=
\lambda_{\min}(\LamT)
-\sup_{(\aT,\mupar)\in\mathcal D}
\lambda_{\max}\left[
\sym\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf g_{\rm em}}{\partial\aT}\right)
\right].
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
If a verified lower bound gives \(\kappa>0\), the reduced electrothermal system is contractive on the certified domain. The Jacobian of \(\mathbf g_{\rm em}\) is obtained by differentiating the small reduced electromagnetic system and the preassembled loss tensors; no finite-difference perturbation is required.

\subsection{Error decomposition}
Let
\begin{align*}
\eta_T&=\text{thermal spatial-reduction/truncation residual},\\
\eta_{\rm em}&=\text{electromagnetic reduced-state residual bound},\\
\eta_{\rm sep}&=\text{constitutive/geometry operator-separation remainder},\\
\eta_{\rm lin}&=\text{reduced linear-solve residual},\\
\eta_{\rm nn}&=\norm{\mathbf R_\theta}.
\end{align*}
Propagating electromagnetic state/operator errors through the loss functional gives a thermal-source perturbation bound
\[
\eta_Q\le C_{Q,u}\eta_{\rm em}+C_{Q,A}\eta_{\rm sep}+C_{Q,\rm lin}\eta_{\rm lin}.
\]
Define
\[
\eta_{\rm tot}=\eta_T+\eta_Q+\eta_{\rm nn}.
\]
For exact initial-condition embedding, the thermal-state error satisfies
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\norm{\aT_\theta(t)-\aT(t)}
\le
\int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\eta_{\rm tot}(s)\,ds
\le
\frac{\sup_{s\le t}\eta_{\rm tot}(s)}{\kappa}.
}
\label{eq:uniform_error}
\end{equation}
Hence the certificate does not deteriorate merely because a large query time is requested. If \(\kappa\le0\), only a finite-time Gr\"onwall bound is claimed; long-time contractive certification is withheld.

\subsection{Output bounds}
For any differentiable output functional \(Q(T,\mathbf u)\),
\[
|Q-Q_\theta|
\le
L_{Q,T}\norm{T-T_\theta}
+L_{Q,u}\norm{\mathbf u-\mathbf u_r}
+\eta_{Q,\rm sep}+\eta_{Q,\rm lin}.
\]
This produces explicit certificates for \(T_{\max}\), \(\mathbf Z\), \(R,L,M\), \(P_{\rm Cu}\), and \(P_{\rm sea}\). The user-facing accuracy target is specified on these engineering outputs; internal ranks and tolerances are then chosen by minimizing online cost subject to the propagated output bound.

\section{Resolution of the three implementation difficulties}

\subsection{Difficulty I: electromagnetic basis without data}
\textbf{Resolved by:} source/constraint Riesz initialization \eqref{eq:V0}, minimum-residual reduced solve \eqref{eq:minres_rom}, worst-case certified residual search \eqref{eq:worst_eta}, and Riesz-lift enrichment \eqref{eq:riesz_lift}. No physical full-order state snapshot is required.

\subsection{Difficulty II: temperature and geometry change the electromagnetic operator}
