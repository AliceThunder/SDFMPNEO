\section{Release-suite matrix}
\label{sec:release-matrix}

A release suite is not a random holdout. It is a locked matrix of physical
regimes designed to falsify the architecture.

\subsection{Electromagnetic release axes}

At minimum, scenes span:
\begin{enumerate}
\item superellipse exponent from ellipse-like to strongly rounded-rectangular;
\item thin to thick conductor cross-sections;
\item low to high skin-depth ratio;
\item weak, moderate, and near-field coupling;
\item coaxial, lateral-offset, orthogonal, and generic random relative poses;
\item unequal coil sizes, pitches, turns, and aspect ratios;
\item low and high end of the declared frequency domain;
\item one, two, and multi-object scenes when supported;
\item weak and strong passive material contrast after media extensions are
      enabled.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Thermal release axes}

The thermal suite spans:
\begin{enumerate}
\item short, intermediate, and asymptotic time scales;
\item localized and distributed loss-channel combinations;
\item different relative positions between heat sources and observation points;
\item weak and strong environmental cooling;
\item temperature-independent and temperature-dependent conductor resistance
      once nonlinear coupling is enabled;
\item current-controlled and circuit/voltage-controlled operation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cross-product policy}

A full Cartesian product is often prohibitively large. The locked suite uses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item pairwise covering arrays across discrete regime bins;
\item explicit worst-case combinations known to be difficult;
\item deterministic random scenes from a fixed hidden seed;
\item hand-constructed analytic benchmark scenes.
\end{enumerate}
No architecture decision may inspect or replace individual hidden release
scenes.

\subsection{Required reports}

For every release cell report at least:
\[
 \epsilon_Z,\quad
 \epsilon_{\rm power},\quad
 \epsilon_q,\quad
 \epsilon_T,\quad
 \eta_{\rm EM},\quad
 m_{\rm correction},
\]
plus passivity, reciprocity, and stability status.

Aggregate metrics include median, 95th percentile, and maximum. Production
gates use per-regime maxima or explicitly declared robust quantiles; a global
average alone is never sufficient.

\subsection{FAST and CERTIFIED gates}

FAST and CERTIFIED are evaluated separately.

FAST gates measure pure surrogate usefulness:
\[
  \text{latency},\quad
  \epsilon_Z,\quad
  \epsilon_T,\quad
  \text{calibrated uncertainty}.
\]

CERTIFIED gates additionally require:
\[
  \eta_{\rm physical},\quad
  \epsilon_{\rm observable}^{\rm bound},\quad
  m_{\rm correction}\le m_{\max}.
\]
A scene may fail FAST accuracy yet pass CERTIFIED after a small correction. A
scene that routinely requires a cold-solve-sized correction fails the
CERTIFIED performance goal even if the final answer is accurate.

\subsection{Teacher release}

The REFERENCE teacher has its own locked convergence suite independent of the
neural model. It must pass before its labels are admitted into training. This
prevents a neural release test from validating against a common biased teacher
without independent evidence.
