\section{Material and environment model}

\subsection{Constitutive interface}

Every material exposes frequency- and temperature-dependent constitutive maps
\[
  \epsilon(\omega,T,\mathbf r),\quad
  \mu(\omega,T,\mathbf r),\quad
  \sigma(\omega,T,\mathbf r),
\]
and thermal maps
\[
  k(T,\mathbf r),\quad
  c_v(T,\mathbf r),\quad
  \rho_m(T,\mathbf r).
\]
Tensor-valued anisotropic laws are allowed. Passive materials must satisfy the
appropriate positivity and dissipation conditions over the declared frequency
and temperature domain.

\subsection{Environment classes}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Environment & Primary representation & Global volume mesh?\\
\midrule
homogeneous unbounded & analytic Green kernel & no\\
layered background & layered Green kernel & no\\
piecewise homogeneous bodies & surface integral equations & no\\
localized continuous contrast & local VIE / adaptive nodes & local only\\
strongly nonlinear material & iterative local constitutive update & local only\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The thermal backend follows the same principle: analytic/Green transfer in
homogeneous regions, boundary operators on interfaces, and localized volumetric
correction only where coefficients vary continuously.

\subsection{Temperature feedback}

Electromagnetic constitutive quantities may depend on the reduced thermal state.
For slowly varying thermal evolution relative to the carrier period,
\[
  Z(t)\approx Z(\omega;\mathcal S,T(t)).
\]
For stronger frequency-domain memory or wideband electrical drive, the
electromagnetic subsystem is replaced by the passive rational state-space model
defined in Section~\ref{sec:port-macro}.

\subsection{Material embeddings for learning}

A neural model must not identify a material solely by a categorical ID.
Material tokens are functions or sampled constitutive descriptors over the
declared operating range. A compact embedding may contain coefficients of a
causal rational fit, selected log-frequency samples, temperature derivatives,
and thermophysical scalars. Two materials with identical constitutive laws must
be indistinguishable to the learned operator.
