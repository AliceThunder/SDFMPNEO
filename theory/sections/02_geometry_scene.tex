\section{Continuous geometry and scene representation}

\subsection{Superelliptic spiral centerline}

A planar superellipse with semi-axes $a,b>0$ and exponent $n\ge 2$ is
parameterized by
\[
 x(\phi)=a\,\operatorname{sgn}(\cos\phi)|\cos\phi|^{2/n},\qquad
 y(\phi)=b\,\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\phi)|\sin\phi|^{2/n}.
\]
A spiral is obtained by allowing the semi-axes to vary monotonically:
\[
 a(\phi)=a_0-\frac{p_x}{2\pi}\phi,\qquad
 b(\phi)=b_0-\frac{p_y}{2\pi}\phi,\qquad
 0\le\phi\le 2\pi N .
\]
The local centerline is
\[
  c_0(\phi)=\begin{bmatrix}x(\phi)&y(\phi)&0\end{bmatrix}^{\T}.
\]
An arbitrary pose $(R,t)\in\SE(3)$ gives
\[
  c(\phi)=Rc_0(\phi)+t.
\]
The continuous exponent $n$ includes ellipse-like coils near $n=2$ and
rounded-rectangle-like coils for larger $n$ without a categorical shape
discontinuity.

\subsection{Finite conductor cross-section}

At each centerline parameter we define a transport frame
\[
  \{e_t(\phi),e_1(\phi),e_2(\phi)\}.
\]
A Bishop/parallel-transport frame is preferred to a Frenet frame because it
remains well behaved at small curvature. A superelliptic cross-section is
\[
 \left|\frac{\xi_1}{w/2}\right|^{m}
 +\left|\frac{\xi_2}{h/2}\right|^{m}\le 1.
\]
The conductor volume is the swept image
\[
  r(\phi,\xi_1,\xi_2)
  =c(\phi)+\xi_1 e_1(\phi)+\xi_2 e_2(\phi).
\]

\subsection{Packages and passive bodies}

Packages are represented by superquadrics, swept profiles, analytic primitives,
or constructive solid geometry. A superellipsoid is defined by
\[
 \left(
   \left|\frac{x}{a}\right|^{2/\epsilon_2}
  +\left|\frac{y}{b}\right|^{2/\epsilon_2}
 \right)^{\epsilon_2/\epsilon_1}
 +\left|\frac{z}{c}\right|^{2/\epsilon_1}=1.
\]
Each object carries its own pose in $\SE(3)$ and one or more material regions.

\subsection{Scene graph}

The scene is a variable-size graph. Node $j$ contains intrinsic features
\[
  h_j^{\mathrm{int}}
  =
  (\text{type},\text{shape parameters},\text{material law},\text{local ports}),
\]
while geometric relations are encoded by relative poses
\[
  R_{ij}=R_i^\T R_j,\qquad
  d_{ij}=R_i^\T(t_j-t_i).
\]
No global Cartesian origin is needed by the learned global operator.

\begin{invariant}[Rigid-scene invariance]
If all objects are transformed by the same $(R_0,t_0)\in\SE(3)$ in an isotropic
unbounded background, port quantities are unchanged. Spatial fields transform
equivariantly through the query coordinates.
\end{invariant}

\subsection{Geometry validity}

A scene validator enforces non-self-intersection, positive cross-section,
clearance rules, package containment rules when requested, and material-region
consistency. These constraints act on continuous geometry, not on mesh quality.
Teacher-specific quadrature or panelization validity is a separate numerical
concern.
