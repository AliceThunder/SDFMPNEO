\section{Scene sampling, symmetry quotient, and coverage}
\label{sec:scene-sampling}

Arbitrary-pose support requires a training and validation measure defined on the
physical scene space rather than on an arbitrary ordered parameter vector.

\subsection{Quotient by global rigid motion}

In an isotropic homogeneous electromagnetic background, a common scene transform
\[
  g_0\cdot\mathcal S
\]
does not change port observables. Therefore training samples are generated on
the quotient space
\[
  \mathfrak S/\SE(3)
\]
whenever the environment shares that symmetry.

For a two-object scene, one object may be fixed as a gauge frame:
\[
  g_1=I,\qquad g_{12}=g_1^{-1}g_2.
\]
For a general scene, a canonical root object or center-of-mass/inertia frame may
be used only for sampling convenience; the neural model itself remains
permutation-aware and relative-pose based.

When the environment breaks global rigid symmetry, for example a layered medium
or fixed wall, the environment frame becomes physical and is retained.

\subsection{Uniform rotation sampling}

Euler-angle components are not sampled independently and uniformly because this
does not produce a uniform distribution on $\SO(3)$ and overweights some
orientations.

For isotropic pose coverage, draw
\[
  q=\frac{g}{\norm g_2},\qquad
  g_k\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
\]
where $q$ is a unit quaternion, identifying $q$ and $-q$. This induces the Haar
measure on $\SO(3)$.

Restricted-orientation domains are specified geometrically, for example by a
cone around a nominal normal plus free twist, and sampled with the correct
surface/solid-angle measure.

\subsection{Relative translation variables}

Raw Cartesian translation is replaced by dimensionless interaction variables:
\[
  \widetilde d_{ij}=\frac{t_j-t_i}{L_{ij}},
\]
where $L_{ij}$ is an intrinsic pair scale such as the geometric mean of coil
diameters.

Sampling is stratified by
\[
  \frac{d_{\rm gap}}{L_{ij}},
  \qquad
  \frac{d_{\rm gap}}{\min(w_i,h_i,w_j,h_j)},
\]
because electromagnetic difficulty changes rapidly near contact. Near-field
regimes receive explicit probability mass instead of relying on uniform volume
sampling, which overwhelmingly produces weakly coupled far configurations.

\subsection{Intrinsic geometry sampling}

Strictly positive size variables are sampled in dimensionless or logarithmic
coordinates when the declared range spans large ratios:
\[
  \ell=\log(L/L_0).
\]
Superellipse exponent, aspect ratio, pitch-to-width ratio,
thickness-to-width ratio, and turn count are sampled directly in physically
valid transformed coordinates. Geometry constraints are enforced before a
candidate reaches the teacher.

A continuous turn count means that terminal endpoints may stop after a
fractional turn; it does not imply a fractional topological conductor.

\subsection{Material and frequency sampling}

Material samples are drawn from constitutive parameter spaces, not only from
named material IDs. Frequency is sampled in
\[
  \log\omega
\]
when several decades are covered, with additional strata near regime
transitions such as
\[
  \min(w,h)/\delta\approx1
\]
or electrical size
\[
  |k|L\approx1.
\]

\subsection{Variable object count}

The training measure is a mixture
\[
  \pi(\mathcal S)
  =
  \sum_{n=1}^{N_{\max}}
  \pi_n\,\pi(\mathcal S\mid N_{\rm obj}=n).
\]
Low-order self/pair data are deliberately overrepresented early because they
are reusable across many larger scenes. Multi-object scenes then train the
many-body residual.

Object count beyond the trained range is marked extrapolative unless the
selected graph architecture and release suite explicitly certify such
generalization.

\subsection{Physics-regime stratification}

Every scene is tagged by dimensionless regime descriptors such as
\[
 \chi_{\rm skin},\quad
 \kappa_{\rm scene},\quad
 d_{\rm gap}/L,\quad
 \text{material contrast},\quad
 N_{\rm obj},\quad
 \text{thermal Biot-like groups}.
\]
Validation and release sets are stratified over these descriptors. A low global
mean error cannot hide a failed near-contact, high-skin, large-rotation, or
strong-contrast regime.

\subsection{Fixed validation cells in regime space}

Before training begins, define immutable validation cells
\[
  \mathcal V_1,\ldots,\mathcal V_K
\]
covering combinations of important regimes. Model selection reports
\[
  \epsilon_k
  =
  \max_{\mathcal S\in\mathcal V_k}
  \epsilon(\mathcal S)
\]
and a declared aggregate. Active learning cannot move scenes between these
cells or replace a difficult cell with easier new samples.

\subsection{Active-learning candidate pool}

Candidate generation uses a broad physical proposal distribution. Acquisition
score combines
\[
 a(\mathcal S)
 =
 w_r\eta_{\rm residual}
 +w_o\eta_{\rm observable}
 +w_u\eta_{\rm uncertainty}
 +w_c\eta_{\rm coverage}.
\]
The coverage term is computed in a dimensionless physics feature space or
learned invariant latent, but it is secondary to actual residual/observable
error. Diversity constraints prevent selecting many nearly identical high-score
candidates.

\subsection{Symmetry tests as zero-cost data}

Common rigid transforms and object relabelings produce exact transformed
answers and are used as invariance/equivariance tests. They need not become
independent expensive truth solves.

For reciprocal identical-object pairs, swapping object/port labels gives exact
matrix permutation relations. These identities are mandatory unit tests for
the data pipeline and network decoder.

\subsection{Extrapolation declaration}

Every artifact stores the support/coverage summary of:
\[
  \{\text{intrinsic geometry},
    \text{relative pose},
    \text{materials},
    \omega,
    T,
    N_{\rm obj}\}.
\]
FAST mode may return an extrapolation warning outside this support.
CERTIFIED mode remains authoritative if the physical correction and observable
certificate succeed.
