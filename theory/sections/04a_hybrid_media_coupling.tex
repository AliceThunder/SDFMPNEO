\section{Hybrid conductor--surface--contrast coupling}
\label{sec:hybrid-media}

\subsection{Block decomposition}

Partition electromagnetic unknowns into conductor, interface, and volumetric
contrast variables:
\[
 x=
 \begin{bmatrix}
  x_c\\x_s\\x_v
 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
The coupled operator is
\[
 \begin{bmatrix}
  A_{cc} & A_{cs} & A_{cv}\\
  A_{sc} & A_{ss} & A_{sv}\\
  A_{vc} & A_{vs} & A_{vv}
 \end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}
  x_c\\x_s\\x_v
 \end{bmatrix}
 =
 \begin{bmatrix}
  B_c\\B_s\\B_v
 \end{bmatrix}i .
 \label{eq:hybrid-block}
\]
Every off-diagonal block is a Green-operator interaction between continuous
supports; no interpolation through a common world grid is required.

\subsection{Surface-equivalence block}

For piecewise homogeneous object interfaces, $x_s$ contains equivalent
tangential electric and magnetic surface currents. A stable transmission
formulation is selected according to constitutive contrast and frequency. The
surface unknown count scales with interface complexity, not object volume.

The implementation must check:
\begin{enumerate}
\item tangential-field transmission residual;
\item jump-condition sign/orientation consistency;
\item reciprocity under reciprocal materials;
\item independence from the arbitrary choice of interface normal convention
      after the corresponding unknown orientation is transformed.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contrast-volume block}

For an inhomogeneous region $\Omega_\Delta$, define a background constitutive
law and contrast operator $\chi$. Schematically,
\[
  E=E_{\rm inc}+\mathcal G_\Delta J_\Delta,\qquad
  J_\Delta=\chi E .
\]
Hence
\[
  (I-\chi\mathcal G_\Delta)J_\Delta
  =
  \chi E_{\rm inc}.
\]
The support of $J_\Delta$ is exactly $\Omega_\Delta$. Adaptive nodes or local
high-order basis functions are refined according to constitutive variation and
field residual, not according to a global Cartesian resolution.

\subsection{Schur elimination}

When interface or contrast unknowns are expensive but port count is small,
Equation~\eqref{eq:hybrid-block} may be reduced by a Schur complement. For
example, eliminating $x_s,x_v$ gives an effective conductor operator
\[
 A_{\rm eff}
 =
 A_{cc}
 -A_{c\eta}A_{\eta\eta}^{-1}A_{\eta c},
 \qquad
 \eta=(s,v).
\]
This is useful for repeated port solves and for defining neural corrections:
the learned model may approximate the low-rank action of the environmental
Schur complement rather than the entire field solution.

\subsection{Material passivity}

For passive constitutive laws, the time-averaged dissipated power computed from
conductor, dielectric, magnetic, and radiation channels must be non-negative.
Each channel is evaluated independently and assembled as
\[
 \Herm Z
 =
 D_c+D_d+D_m+D_{\rm out}.
\]
This channel decomposition is retained in truth data and in the learned
structured decoder. A small channel is never inferred solely by subtracting two
large independently fitted terms.

\subsection{Layered environments}

For planar or otherwise analytically separable backgrounds, layered Green
functions may replace explicit interfaces. The scene semantics remain
unchanged: this is a backend acceleration selected by the environment kernel,
not a different public geometry representation.

\subsection{Moving objects}

Rigid motion changes only geometry-dependent interaction blocks. Intrinsic self
blocks of a rigid object in a homogeneous isotropic medium are invariant under
common rigid motion and are cacheable. Pair blocks depend on relative pose.
This separation is a primary mechanism for fast REFERENCE data generation over
arbitrary poses.
